2. The data in plain language

Think of a US office-supplies store that sells online. Every time a customer places an order, the order can contain several products. Each row in the Orders sheet is one product line in one order. For example, "order US-2021-112326 contained 2 packs of binder combs, sold for $3.54, and we lost $5.49 on it".

Neither small sheet is needed for the requirements. I'll load People anyway so each region's card can show its manager. Returns stays out for now.

CheckResultWhat we'll do
Missing valuesNone. Every row has dates, location, product and numbers.Nothing.

3. What the problem statement wants, simply

One dashboard page that answers: "How did each region do in the year I pick, and how does that compare with the year before?"

The controls

  • Year filter: pick 2021, 2022, 2023, or 2024. Everything on the page follows it.
  • Metric switch: pick Sales, Profit, or Quantity. The KPI cards and their small charts switch to that metric.

The four region KPI cards (Central, East, South, West)

Each card shows the same four things for its region:

  1. The selected metric's value for the chosen year (for example, Central Sales 2024 = $147,098).
  2. Sales for the previous year (PY), for comparison (Central Sales 2023 = $147,429), plus the change.
  3. A small monthly bar chart (a "bar sparkline") with 12 bars for Jan–Dec of the chosen year,
  4. …and an average line across it, so you can spot the months above or below average at a glance.

Sales by State

  • A bubble map of the US (plus those 3 Canadian provinces), where a bigger bubble means more sales.
  • A bar chart next to it listing every state, which you can sort by sales high-to-low or low-to-high.

The CY vs PY table

A grid with 9 numbers per row: CY (current = selected year), PY (previous year) and YoY (the % change between them), for each of Sales, Profit, and Quantity.

For example, for the whole company in 2024: CY Sales $733,948, PY Sales $609,861, YoY Sales +20.3%.

Note: if you pick 2021, every PY and YoY value will be blank, because there is no 2020 data. That's expected, not a bug.

4. How we'll get there

  1. Load the Orders and People sheets into Sales.pbix and give every column the right type.
  2. Add a Date table (Jan 2021 – Dec 2024) linked to Order Date. This is what makes "previous year" work.
  3. Add a Metric table with Sales / Profit / Quantity, to drive the metric switch.
  4. Write the DAX measures: CY / PY / YoY for each metric, plus the dynamic "selected metric" measures and their titles and colours.
  5. Build the visuals on the report page, following the layout above.
  6. Write the insights.

Steps 1–4 (the data model) I can do directly inside Power BI Desktop. Step 5 (placing visuals) has to be done by hand in the Desktop canvas, because my tools can edit the model but not the report page. plan.md has a click-by-click guide for it.
